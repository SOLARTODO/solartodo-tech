A 176-pole corridor at 35 m spacing represents about 6.16 km of frontage, with every 10 m pole carrying lighting, a 7 kW AC charger, PTZ security, 5G, and environmental sensing in one streetscape asset.

## Deployment Basis for Buenos Aires

The technical fit is driven by density and right-of-way pressure. Buenos Aires has 3,121,707 residents within 205.9 km², approximately 15,161 people/km². In that setting, separate posts for lighting, cameras, telecom, EV charging, public address, SOS, and air-quality monitoring can compete for the same sidewalk zone. A consolidated smart pole configuration reduces independent roadside cabinets while keeping service functions accessible for municipal, transport, and utility teams.

The reference deployment uses 176 units installed at 35 m intervals. That spacing gives an indicative corridor length of 6.16 km, suitable for transport routes, retail streets, waterfront works, or redevelopment zones connected to the broader Buenos Aires metropolitan market, which exceeds 13.9 million people when the city and 24 surrounding partidos are included. The pole class is adapted as a 10 m urban configuration from SOLARTODO's grid-powered smart streetlight family, using 220/380 V AC input instead of a standalone off-grid design.

Local environmental conditions also affect the hardware package. Buenos Aires is planned here as a humid, storm-exposed site with annual precipitation around 1,250 mm and frequent wind events associated with Pampero and Sudestada patterns. The proposed structure should therefore use corrosion-controlled steel, hot-dip galvanizing or equivalent protective treatment, sealed service doors, IP-rated electronics, and internal cable routing.

## Pole Architecture and Electrical Loads

The main structure is a 10 m octagonal tapered steel pole. Its section changes from Ø45 cm at the base to Ø15 cm near the top, leaving enough lower volume for equipment access while keeping the upper profile suitable for lighting and wireless devices. The lower 2.2 m is the integrated EV charging cabinet, so the charger becomes part of the pole envelope rather than a separate roadside unit.

Lighting is specified as two 80 W LED heads per pole, giving 160 W installed lighting power. At 150 lm/W and 4000 K, the configuration targets neutral-white roadway and pedestrian lighting without changing the pole into a high-mast asset. The LED advertising display is a portrait P4 module measuring 960 x 1920 mm, with brightness specified above 5500 cd/m² for outdoor visibility.

## Interfaces, Standards, and Maintainability

The EV interface should use IEC 62196-2 Type 2 hardware with a 5 m coiled cable and OCPP 1.6J for charger communication. A 7 kW AC charger aligns with curbside dwell-time use cases and avoids the footprint, heat, and grid service requirements of high-power DC equipment. The 5G component is specified as an NR n78 4T4R MIMO small cell mounted at 8.7 m, with an indicative 200 m urban coverage radius depending on obstruction, backhaul, and operator planning.

Standards alignment should be explicit in procurement documents: IEC 60598 for luminaires, GB/T 37024 for smart pole requirements, and IEC 62196-2 for the EV connector interface. For a SOLARTODO delivery package, acceptance items include dimensional conformity, corrosion protection, sealed access, AC input compatibility at 220/380 V, OCPP 1.6J charger integration, lighting photometric data, display brightness, and mount locations for PTZ camera, air-quality sensor, SOS, public address, and telecom equipment.
