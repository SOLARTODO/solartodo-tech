## 15 km 10 kV Feeder Using 363 Galvanized Steel Tubular Poles

A representative 15 km community distribution line around Sao Paulo can be configured with **363 single-circuit steel tubular poles**, each **8 m** high, laid out on an average **40 m span**. This is a short-span overhead arrangement for peri-urban, rural, service-road, farm, and community load connections where an 8 m pole class is sufficient and a larger 10-35 kV main-corridor structure is unnecessary.

The profile is not a high-voltage transmission corridor. It is a compact **10 kV distribution** configuration using tapered Q345 steel poles with hot-dip galvanizing, concrete foundations, and grounding at every structure. For a procurement team, the key distinction is that the supplied height, span, conductor loading, and clearance assumptions belong to low-voltage or community distribution duty; standard 10-35 kV main distribution corridors more commonly move to **12-18 m** poles and **80-150 m** spans.

The civil basis uses **concrete base foundations** rather than direct embedment alone. Grounding is specified at each pole, which should be verified against soil resistivity, utility practice, and fault-clearing requirements before final bill-of-material release. Clearance practice references **GB 50061** for overhead distribution alignment, while short-circuit mechanical effects can be checked under **IEC 60865** where applicable to the electrical layout.

## Sao Paulo Application Context and Execution Notes

Sao Paulo combines dense metropolitan demand with outer districts and agricultural municipalities that still need short distribution extensions. The city has about **11.45 million** residents in **IBGE 2022** figures, while the wider metropolitan area exceeds **20 million** people. That load geography creates both urban feeder reinforcement and edge-of-grid connection work, but it does not mean every extension needs 35 kV, 110 kV, or taller lattice/tubular structures.

The 8 m, 40 m-span arrangement fits constrained rights-of-way and localized loads where short clearances and frequent pole positions are acceptable. In these conditions, equipment matching matters more than catalog height alone: pole taper, galvanizing quality, base plate or anchor details, conductor tension, insulator geometry, and grounding layout all affect whether the package can be erected without field redesign. SOLARTODO should therefore be assessed against the complete configuration: **363 Q345 galvanized poles**, **ACSR 50 conductor**, **16 kN** maximum tension, **0.8 m** phase spacing, **0.5 m** insulators, **25 m/s** wind design, and **5 m** clearance target.

A typical 15 km rollout would proceed through survey, foundation works, pole erection, conductor stringing, and energization. For this scale, field execution is commonly planned across **8-16 weeks**, depending on access, permits, outage windows, and foundation curing.
