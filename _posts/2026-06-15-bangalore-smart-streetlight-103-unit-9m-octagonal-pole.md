## 103-unit corridor at 32 m spacing
A practical Bangalore streetlighting deployment at this scale is a **103-unit corridor** laid out at **32 m spacing**, which yields roughly **3.3 km** of street frontage coverage. For an urban road environment, the recommended structure is a **9 m octagonal tapered steel pole** rather than a highway mast. The pole geometry is specified with a **45 cm base diameter** and **15 cm top diameter**, matching city-road loading and equipment integration needs.

The functional stack on each pole combines lighting, power generation, storage, charging, and smart-city payloads. The baseline electrical package uses **2×200 W monocrystalline solar panels**, a **500 W Darrieus H-type vertical-axis wind turbine**, and a **10 kWh LFP battery** with **MPPT** control and **grid backup**. This hybrid arrangement is suitable where solar is available but monsoon variability and nighttime demand require stored energy and utility support.

### Core pole and electrical specification
The lighting head uses **twin 1.5 m arms** with a **+8° tilt** and **2×80 W LED luminaires**. The luminaires are rated at **150 lm/W** and **4000 K**, which places the output in a neutral white range appropriate for urban streets and mixed-use corridors. The lower section of the pole, specifically the **bottom 2.2 m**, is reserved as the EV charging cabinet. That cabinet houses a **7 kW single-gun AC charger** with a **Type 2 connector** and **OCPP 1.6J** communication support.

## Integrated street furniture payloads
Beyond lighting and charging, the pole is configured as a multi-service node. The public-safety and monitoring package includes an **8 MP 180° fisheye camera**, an **8-parameter environmental sensor**, a **30 W IP audio column**, and a **one-press SOS button**. Connectivity is handled by a **flush-mounted WiFi 6 access point** installed at **8.7 m**, with support for up to **256 devices** and a peak throughput of **1.8 Gbps**.

For procurement teams, the important point is that the pole is not a single-purpose luminaire. It is a combined infrastructure asset that must accommodate lighting optics, renewable generation, battery mass, EV charging hardware, communications electronics, and public-safety interfaces within one structural envelope. ## Deployment fit for Bangalore streets
Bangalore’s dense arterial and collector roads are better served by a **9 m urban pole class** than by oversized highway structures. The 32 m spacing pattern supports continuous lighting coverage while leaving room for charger access, sensor placement, and maintenance clearances. The hybrid power architecture is also aligned with mixed grid and renewable operation, since the **10 kWh LFP** battery can buffer load while the **grid backup** maintains service continuity.

The result is a streetlight node that can support illumination, EV top-up, surveillance, environmental sensing, emergency communication, and wireless access from one pole line.
