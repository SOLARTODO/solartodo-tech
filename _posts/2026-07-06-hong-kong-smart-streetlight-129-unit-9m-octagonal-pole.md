## 129-Unit Corridor Baseline for Dense Hong Kong Streets

A 4.1 km Hong Kong urban corridor can be planned around **approximately 129 smart streetlight poles** when the spacing target is **32 m**, which works out to about **31 poles per km**. For procurement teams, that density is the useful starting point: it defines civil works quantity, feeder planning, charger count, communications coverage, and operations scope before vendor selection.

The proposed asset is a **9 m octagonal tapered steel hybrid pole** designed for lighting, EV charging, generation, storage, communications, and public information equipment in one structure. Hong Kong’s street conditions make the compact format relevant: the territory has about **7.5 million residents** in roughly **1,114 km²**, so curbside infrastructure cannot be treated as single-purpose hardware. Roadside equipment also needs to account for annual rainfall above **2,300 mm** and tropical cyclone exposure, making corrosion protection, drainage, sealed enclosures, and wind loading part of the base specification rather than optional upgrades.

## Engineering Configuration

The pole integrates an **11 kW Type 2 AC charger** into the lower **2.2 m** section. This is not a separate roadside bollard; the charger cabinet is formed within the same continuous steel pole body. The charging profile is aligned with parking dwell times of **1-4 hours**, rather than rapid-charge sessions of **15-30 minutes**, which makes it suitable for commercial streets, residential frontage, and municipal parking edges.

Power support combines a **400 W Gorlov-type vertical-axis wind turbine**, **2×150 W PV modules**, and **15 kWh LFP battery backup**. The LFP storage is sized for lighting resilience, communications continuity, and partial support of auxiliary loads, while grid supply remains the practical basis for consistent 11 kW AC charging.

Lighting is specified as **2×80 W LED** at **150 lm/W** and **4000 K**, giving approximately **24,000 lm per pole**. This output level fits distributor-road and mixed-use street applications where lighting, EV access, and digital devices share the same right-of-way.

## Communications, Safety, and Rollout Sequence

For connected-street functions, the proposed package includes **WiFi 6** capacity up to **1.8 Gbps** and **256 devices**, plus a **4 MP camera** with **50 m IR** coverage. A **TCP/IP public address system** can be included for municipal announcements and incident response workflows. These devices should be reviewed as electrical loads, network endpoints, and maintenance items, not as separate add-ons outside the pole bill of materials.

A practical municipal deployment of about **129 units** would normally be staged over **12-24 weeks**. The sequence should cover design review, utility coordination, civil preparation, foundations, pole erection, charger configuration, **OCPP** setup, network commissioning, and final acceptance checks. For Hong Kong streets, the acceptance process should also verify enclosure sealing, cable routing, cabinet access, drainage path, wind-load assumptions, and maintainable clearances around the integrated charger section.
